# Experiments

This document describes how each table in the paper was produced, as stated in
the paper, and what in this repository relates to it.

Per-trial records for the evaluation tables are not part of this release.
`data/raw/` contains August development logs recorded with an earlier version
of the supervisor; they document the log format and can be replayed through
the detector, but do not reproduce the paper's tables.

`analysis/paper_statistics.py` recomputes the interval estimates and paired
tests from the counts printed in the paper. It takes those counts as input; it
does not derive them from data.

## Common protocol (Sec. IV-A)

* One fixed VLA checkpoint in all experiments.
* Five held-out test objects, excluded from VLA training, detector calibration
  and controller development: cube, fish, soy sauce bottle, thermos lid, hand
  cream tube.
* A trial starts from an initialized scene and may contain several closures. A
  retry requires policy-commanded reopening followed by a new closure.
* Task completion is recorded independently of the supervisor.
* Randomized, interleaved matched blocks under a shared execution budget.
  Within a block all conditions start from a common initial configuration.
* A trial ends at successful placement, after 60 s, or after three arm
  approaches, whichever comes first. No started trial is excluded.
* Paired comparisons: two-sided exact McNemar tests with Holm correction over
  the pairwise contrasts reported within each experiment.

## Table II: main comparison

75 matched blocks, 15 per held-out object, three conditions.

| Condition | How to run it here |
|---|---|
| Base VLA | `scripts/run_agent.py --no-agent` |
| VLA + unconditional close-to-stall | `scripts/run_agent.py --variant U-stall` |
| Supervisor | `scripts/run_agent.py` (variant `S-full`) |

The close-to-stall baseline pauses the body reference at every terminal grasp
and advances the fingers until the position-stall condition, the travel limit
or the shared 4.0 s timeout. It has no contact-count verdict and no
verdict-dependent release.

| Object | VLA | VLA + stall | Supervisor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cube | 9/15 | 11/15 | 13/15 |
| Fish | 9/15 | 11/15 | 13/15 |
| Soy sauce bottle | 11/15 | 12/15 | 14/15 |
| Thermos lid | 7/15 | 8/15 | 10/15 |
| Hand cream tube | 10/15 | 12/15 | 13/15 |
| Pooled | 46/75 | 54/75 | 63/75 |
| Rate | 61.3% | 72.0% | 84.0% |
| 95% Wilson CI | 50.0-71.5 | 61.0-80.9 | 74.1-90.6 |

Discordant blocks: supervisor against base VLA 20/3 (Holm-corrected p = 0.001),
supervisor against close-to-stall 11/2 (p = 0.045), close-to-stall against
base VLA 12/4 (p = 0.077, not significant). The supervisor intervened in 27 of
75 trials with 36 correction episodes; the unconditional baseline produced 101
extension episodes.

## Table III: component ablation

100 matched blocks, five variants. All are switches in `config.py`.

| Variant | Meaning | Completion | Corrections |
|---|---|---|---|
| U-stall | every closure, position stall only, no verdict | 72/100 | 135 |
| U-full | every closure, full detector and verdict logic | 84/100 | 134 |
| S-position | selective, no motor-effort evidence | 79/100 | 49 |
| S-retain | selective, no verdict-dependent release | 77/100 | 47 |
| S-full | the complete selective supervisor | 86/100 | 46 |

S-full against U-stall: Holm-corrected p = 0.047. U-full against U-stall is not
resolved after correction (p = 0.091). S-full and U-full do not differ in
completion (p = 0.804, paired 95% CI -5.8 to +9.8 points) while correction
episodes drop from 134 to 46, a 65.7% reduction. The contrasts of S-full with
S-position and S-retain are not resolved (p = 0.378 and 0.235).

The paired tests of this table need per-block outcomes and cannot be
recomputed from the published marginals.

## Table IV: pose perturbation

The same five objects under nominal poses, horizontal translations within
20 mm and yaw changes within 15 degrees, 50 matched trials per regime and
condition.

| Regime | VLA | VLA + stall | Supervisor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nominal | 32/50 | 37/50 | 43/50 |
| Translation | 25/50 | 32/50 | 39/50 |
| Yaw | 23/50 | 30/50 | 37/50 |
| Pooled | 80/150 | 99/150 | 119/150 |

Pooled, the supervisor exceeds close-to-stall in 26/6 discordant blocks
(p < 0.001). The per-regime contrasts at 50 trials are underpowered. The
result shows persistence of the benefit within the tested range, not pose
invariance.

## Diagnostic trials and Fig. 3

70 diagnostic trials produced 85 correction events: 46 FORMED, 26 CROOKED and
13 EMPTY. These are internal decisions, not contact-detection accuracy,
because independent per-finger contact labels do not exist. The stapler gave
FORMED in 8/8 events, the ball in 5/8 and the soft ball in 0/6, although video
showed the soft ball enclosed in all six.

Fig. 3 of the paper is not regenerated by this repository.

## Table V: retention audit

120 independent standardized closures. The grasp is retained for a 5 cm
diagnostic lift and a 2 s hold, which gives a retention label independent of
the verdict.

| Support predicate | Retained | Not retained | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| At least three | 70 | 6 | 76 |
| Below three | 18 | 26 | 44 |

Retention given acceptance 92.1%; NPV 59.1%. On held-out objects, retention
given acceptance is 88.9% and 25.6% of retainable configurations are falsely
rejected.

`analysis/retention_audit.py` computes these rates from a per-closure CSV, or
from the four table cells with `--counts 70 6 18 26`. The held-out split needs
per-closure records.

## Table VI: thin-walled cup

Five trials per condition. The policy receives the same task instruction in
every condition; handling cues go only to the interaction parser
(`--handling-cue`), and the VLM receives the task without them.

| Mode selected by | Intact | Damaged | Compliant active |
|---|---|---|---|
| No selection | 0 | 5 | 0/5 |
| Instruction cue | 5 | 0 | 5/5 |
| VLM from image | 4 | 1 | 4/5 |
| Instruction + VLM | 5 | 0 | 5/5 |
| Always compliant | 5 | 0 | 5/5 |

In the one damaged VLM trial the compliant mode was selected after closure had
begun. On three additional rigid objects the nominal mode was selected in
14/15 trials. The cup is a training object, and always-compliant control
performs equally well, so the experiment shows routing, not an advantage over
always-compliant execution and not held-out fragile-object generalization.

## What can be run from this repository

| Command | Output |
|---|---|
| `python analysis/paper_statistics.py` | Wilson intervals, McNemar and Holm p-values and derived rates from the published counts |
| `python analysis/retention_audit.py --counts 70 6 18 26` | rates of Table V from its cells |
| `python analysis/compute_verdicts.py data/raw/verdicts_left_*.csv` | eq. (8) applied to the August per-finger flags |
| `python scripts/replay_logs.py data/raw/ramp_left_*.csv` | this detector replayed over August correction episodes |
| `python scripts/figures/fig_event.py <ramp csv> -a <n>` | one correction event from an August log |
| `pytest` | unit tests of the supervisor logic on a simulated finger model |
